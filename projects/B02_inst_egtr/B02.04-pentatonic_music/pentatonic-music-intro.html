🎵 Stage 2 · Pentatonic Music

Stage 1 was geography: where the notes are. This stage is the other half — what to do with them.

The split is deliberate and the order is the user's own analogy: alphabet, then vocabulary, then sentences. Stage 1 was the alphabet. It's a legitimate place to start and a terrible place to stop.

The failure this stage exists to prevent: knowing five boxes cold and still sounding like someone practising scales.

That failure is extremely common, and it isn't a knowledge problem. Players who hit it can find every note instantly and still produce continuous, shapeless runs — because nobody ever told them the missing skills are rhythm, space, repetition and resolution, none of which are on a fretboard diagram.

The one thing to carry through all five pages​

More notes is never the answer.
Every page here makes you play FEWER notes than you want to.

Page 01 caps you at one note. Page 02 caps you at one phrase. Page 04 caps you at six notes per bar. That constraint is the method — it's the only reliable way to make arrivals audible, and arrivals are what a listener actually hears.

They are ordered by how often home moves: never, never, once per track, four times per cycle — then, only at the end, new notes. Do not reorder this. Adding notes before you can shape a phrase makes the phrase worse, not better.

What you need before starting​

Stage 1 finished — specifically drill 4.6: name any scale note, find every instance in under thirty seconds. If finding C in box 1 still takes a beat of searching, that beat is stolen from everything on these pages.

A backing track you can loop. Every page here needs one. Record yourself playing Am for four minutes if you have nothing else — the point is a harmonic reference from outside your own head, per file 02.

A recorder. Every test on these pages is a listening test, and you cannot listen and play at the same time.

How to practise this stage​

Differently from Stage 1, and this matters. Stage 1 drills had right answers, so you could self-mark. These don't. The routine that works:

play 60 s   →   listen back   →   name ONE thing to fix   →   play 60 s again

One thing. Not a list. The recording will offer you five problems and you will fix none of them if you try for all five.
