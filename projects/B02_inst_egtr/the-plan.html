The Plan

Everything in this section is material. This page is the order, for one specific starting point.

The assumption: the five pentatonic shapes are solid, barre chords are shaky, and there are ten to fifteen minutes a day rather than an hour. If that isn't you, the Adjusting this section at the bottom says what to move.

Where the gap actually is​

Knowing the pentatonic shapes is further along than it feels, because those five shapes already contain most of what comes next. What they don't contain is any information about harmony — which chord is playing, and which notes belong to it. That's the gap, and it's why the next steps are chord-shaped rather than scale-shaped.

Steps 1 and 2 are new work. Step 3 is re-seeing what you already own, and it's where the payoff is.

The mental model: three layers, one hand position​

This is the idea from How To FINALLY Unlock The Guitar Fretboard, checked against box 1 of A minor pentatonic — the shape you already know.

Layer 1 — the box. Frets 5–8, the twelve notes you can already play. Below, the chord tones are named and the plain circles are the other two notes, the 4th (D) and the ♭7 (G).

   e  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
   B  │──E──┼─────┼─────┼──○──│
   G  │──C──┼─────┼──○──┼─────│
   D  │──○──┼─────┼──A──┼─────│
   A  │──○──┼─────┼──E──┼─────│
   E  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
         5     6     7     8

Eight of those twelve notes are A, C or E — the A minor chord. The box is the chord plus two extra notes.

Layer 2 — the chord. The A minor barre chord at fret 5 uses six of those same notes, and never leaves the box.

   e  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   B  │──E──┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   G  │──C──┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   D  │─────┼─────┼──A──┼─────│
   A  │─────┼─────┼──E──┼─────│
   E  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼─────│
         5     6     7     8

Layer 3 — the triad. That's it: the chord tones marked as landing places. There's no new shape to learn here. The three layers are one shape read three ways.

LayerWhat it's forWhere it comes from
Barre chordrhythm playing, and the anchor for the other twostep 1
Pentatonic boximprovising with no wrong notesalready done
Chord toneslanding on the chord when it changesseeing, not learning

What changes when the chord changes​

The layer that moves is the third one, and only the third one. Over Am the landing notes are A C E:

   e  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
   B  │──E──┼─────┼─────┼──○──│
   G  │──C──┼─────┼──○──┼─────│
   D  │──○──┼─────┼──A──┼─────│
   A  │──○──┼─────┼──E──┼─────│
   E  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
         5     6     7     8

Over C they are C E G. Same box, same hand position, nothing to re-finger:

   e  │──○──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
   B  │──E──┼─────┼─────┼──G──│
   G  │──C──┼─────┼──○──┼─────│
   D  │──G──┼─────┼──○──┼─────│
   A  │──○──┼─────┼──E──┼─────│
   E  │──○──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
         5     6     7     8

Five positions keep their job, because C and E belong to both chords. What changes is that the three As stop being landing notes and the two Gs start. For your hand, the whole chord change is: swap A for G.

"Landing" means the note you arrive on as the new chord starts, held long enough to be heard as an arrival. The other box notes aren't wrong — A over a C chord is the 6th and sounds good — they just don't announce the change. As everywhere else in these notes, duration is what decides.

One warning that saves confusion: Am and C are the hardest pair to hear this on, because they share two of their three notes. Use a progression with more contrast to test yourself. Taking Am F C G from Playing Over Changes:

ChordLand onPlaces available in box 1
AmA C E8
FF A C6 — the box has no F
CC E G7
GG B D4 — the box has no B

That last row is why the G chord sounds vague from this box, which is exactly what that page says. The box can follow three of these four chords well.

The drill: loop two chords and play only the landing notes, one per chord, for a few minutes. It sounds bare, and it teaches your ear what "arriving" feels like. Then open up and use the whole box, on the one condition that you are on a landing note whenever the chord turns over.

The five moves, in order​

1. Barre chords — four shapes​

Major and minor, root on the low E string and on the A string. This is the only genuinely new memorisation on the list, and everything else depends on it.

Why first: it unlocks steps 2 and 3, it's what lets you play songs with other people, and it's the layer that tells you where the harmony is.

2. The ten-minute routine​

From I Stopped Memorizing Chords and Did This Instead: walk a scale up one string, play the chord that belongs to each degree, and at each shape name what every string is playing.

Why second: it's the cheapest way to convert four shapes into a working vocabulary, and it teaches intervals as a side effect.

3. The overlay​

For each barre chord position: see the box and the chord tones with it, every time, until they stop being three things.

Why third: it costs almost nothing once steps 1 and 2 exist, and it's what turns the pentatonic from a scale you run into a scale you can steer.

4. Following the changes​

Loop two chords. Improvise in the box. Land on a chord tone of the new chord as it arrives. This is the whole goal, and it's the first step where a listener could tell the difference.

5. Fill the box​

Only after step 4 feels normal. Two notes per octave turn the minor pentatonic into a full seven-note scale — B and F for A natural minor:

   e  │──●──┼─────┼──✚──┼──○──┼─────│
   B  │──○──┼──✚──┼─────┼──○──┼─────│
   G  │──○──┼─────┼──○──┼─────┼──○──│
   D  │──○──┼─────┼──●──┼─────┼──✚──│
   A  │──○──┼─────┼──○──┼──✚──┼─────│
   E  │──●──┼─────┼──✚──┼──○──┼─────│
         5     6     7     8     9

Circles and dots are the box you already know; the crosses are the two new notes.

Three things worth noticing in that picture:

  • Every string now has exactly three notes. Filling the box produces a three-notes-per-string shape on its own — you don't have to learn that system separately, it's what the box becomes.
  • The shape reaches fret 9 on two strings. Four frets became five, which is the natural hand span described in Did You Learn Guitar Scales The Hard Way?.
  • The two notes you add decide the mode. B and F give A natural minor; B and F♯ give Dorian; B♭ and F give Phrygian. Same box underneath.

How to learn any shape​

The order matters more than the material, and it's the argument of The Fretboard Was Confusing Until I Did This:

  1. Rote first. Play it until the hand runs it unsupervised. Don't analyse yet.
  2. Picture it. Away from the guitar, close your eyes and see it. If it has to be reconstructed, it isn't learned.
  3. Read it. Now name what each finger is playing — root, ♭3, 5th.
  4. Use it. Put it in a song this week, or it won't stay.

One shape at a time. One a week is a good rate, and slower is fine.

The daily session​

MinutesWhatNotes
0–3One Stage 1 drill, slowwherever you left off
3–10The ten-minute routinethis is the engine of the plan
10–15Play musica real song, not an exercise

The last block is the one that gets cut and the one that matters most: it's where everything else gets stored. Once a week, record the last block — without a recording there's no feedback loop, and that's the actual problem with teaching yourself.

Checkpoints​

Move on when the right-hand column is true, not when the calendar says so.

StepDone when
1 · Barre chordsyou can change between them in time, without looking
2 · Routineyou can say which interval each string plays while holding the chord
3 · Overlayseeing a barre chord brings the box with it, unasked
4 · Changesa listener can hear the chord change in your line, not just in the backing
5 · Fill the boxthe two added notes sound like colours you chose, not passing accidents

What to leave alone for now​

Not because it's bad — because it costs more than it returns at this point.

MaterialCome back when
Three notes per string as a systemafter step 5, where it arrives on its own
CAGED as a systemprobably never, formally; steps 1 and 3 give you most of it
Modes as seven shapesafter step 5, and start from the two-note swap
212 arpeggioswhen everything you play sounds too even
Chord theoryas reference, when a chord name you don't know turns up

Adjusting this​

  • If barre chords are already solid, skip step 1, start at step 2, and expect step 3 to take a week rather than a month.
  • If the pentatonic shapes aren't solid yet, do Stage 1 first and come back. Nothing here works without them.
  • If there's more time available, add repertoire, not more systems — the bottleneck at this stage is having somewhere to put what you know.
  • If a step stalls for more than two weeks, it's usually the step before it that isn't finished.

Where this goes next​

Step 4 is the front door to Stage 3, and Guide Tones is the refinement of it: not just a chord tone, but the one carrying the most information. That's the next real jump, and it's worth arriving there with steps 1–4 already automatic.
