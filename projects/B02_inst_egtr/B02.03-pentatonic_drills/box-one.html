Box One

One shape, frets 5–8. Everything on this page is that shape and the three root notes inside it.

The order here is deliberate and slightly unusual: the roots come before the shape is fluent. Most material teaches the box as a fingering and adds note knowledge later, which produces a player who can run the pattern and can't use it.

   e  │──●──┼─────┼─────┼──○──│
   B  │──○──┼─────┼─────┼──○──│
   G  │──○──┼─────┼──○──┼─────│
   D  │──○──┼─────┼──●──┼─────│
   A  │──○──┼─────┼──○──┼─────│
   E  │──●──┼─────┼─────┼──○──│
         5     6     7     8

Drill 1.1 · The shape​

Ascend and descend, two notes per string, low E up to high e and back.

Fingering: index finger on fret 5, ring finger on fret 7, little finger on fret 8. Keep it consistent — a fixed fingering is what lets the shape transpose later without re-deciding.

Test: four times in a row, clean, at a slow tempo, with no buzzes. If you can't get four consecutive clean passes, slow down rather than repeat.

Practise this clean, not distorted. Gain hides exactly the unevenness you're trying to find here — see Gear as a Variable.

Drill 1.2 · Roots only​

Now remove everything except the three As:

   e  │──●──┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   B  │─────┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   G  │─────┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   D  │─────┼─────┼──●──┼─────│
   A  │─────┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   E  │──●──┼─────┼─────┼─────│
         5     6     7     8

Three notes: low E string fret 5, D string fret 7, high e string fret 5. Play only these, up and down, until the triangle they form is automatic.

Test: play the three roots with your eyes closed, no hunting.

This drill looks trivial and is the most important one on the page. The roots are what turn a shape into a key.

Drill 1.3 · Root landing​

Run the full box (drill 1.1), and stop on a root whenever you decide to — not at a planned place. The point is to be able to exit onto home from anywhere in the shape.

Progression:

  1. Stop on any root.
  2. Stop on a specific root (the D-string one).
  3. Stop on a root within two notes of deciding to.

Test: someone claps, and you land on a root within two notes. Alone: set a metronome to a slow click and land on a root on every fourth click.

Drill 1.4 · Name every note​

Same box, but say each note out loud as you play it:

   e  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
   B  │──E──┼─────┼─────┼──G──│
   G  │──C──┼─────┼──D──┼─────│
   D  │──G──┼─────┼──A──┼─────│
   A  │──D──┼─────┼──E──┼─────│
   E  │──A──┼─────┼─────┼──C──│
         5     6     7     8

Slowly. Speaking is the point — it forces retrieval of the name rather than the finger position, which is the exact weakness that shape-based thinking builds in.

Note the box contains only five distinct names — A C D E G — appearing twelve times. Learn where each name lives, not twelve separate facts.

Test: name any position in the box without playing it. "D string, fret 7?" → "A."

Drill 1.5 · The octave ladder​

The three roots are not three random As. They're three consecutive octaves:

   e  │──●──┼─────┼─────┼─────│   ← A4
   B  │─────┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   G  │─────┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   D  │─────┼─────┼──●──┼─────│   ← A3
   A  │─────┼─────┼─────┼─────│
   E  │──●──┼─────┼─────┼─────│   ← A2
         5     6     7     8

Play A2 → A3 → A4 → A3 → A2, listening to it as one rising and falling line rather than three positions.

Two things this installs:

  • The octave shape itself — "up two strings, up two frets" is a movable shape you'll use to find notes everywhere on the neck, well beyond this scale.
  • Two octaves of range inside a four-fret box. Useful to know when a phrase feels cramped: it isn't, you're just staying in the middle.

Test: play the ladder, then find the same octave shape starting from a different note in the box.

Running this page​

A ten-minute version, if you only do one thing:

2 min   1.2  roots only
4 min   1.1  the shape, clean, slow
2 min   1.3  root landing
2 min   1.4  naming, out loud

Drill 1.5 fits naturally once 1.2 is automatic. Do not move to page 02 because you're bored — move when 1.3 works reliably. Everything later depends on knowing where home is.

Takeaway​

  • Box 1 is frets 5–8, two notes per string, fixed fingering 1–3–4.
  • Roots before fluency: the three As (E/5, D/7, e/5) come first.
  • The box has only five note names (A C D E G) in twelve places.
  • The three roots form an octave ladder A2–A3–A4 — two octaves inside four frets, and a movable octave shape for the whole neck.
  • Practise clean; gain hides the unevenness this page exists to expose.
  • Move on when root landing is reliable, not when you're bored.
