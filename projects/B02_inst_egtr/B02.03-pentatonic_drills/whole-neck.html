The Whole Neck

The remaining four boxes, and the map they're windows onto.

By now you can play box 1, travel along a string, and cross a seam. That's most of the difficulty. What's left is mainly bookkeeping — and one genuinely useful piece of structure that makes the bookkeeping small.

Drill 4.1 · The other four boxes, roots first​

Same rule as page 01: find the roots before drilling the shape.

Test, per box: find its roots in under three seconds, then play the shape twice cleanly.

Take one box per session, not four. There is no prize for speed here and the shapes interfere with each other when learned all at once.

Drill 4.2 · The root spine​

This is the most useful thing on the page, and it's usually not taught.

List every box's roots in order and something falls out — consecutive boxes share a root:

box 1    E/5     D/7     e/5
box 2            D/7     B/10          ← shares D/7 with box 1
box 3                    B/10   A/12   ← shares B/10 with box 2
box 4                           A/12   G/14    ← shares A/12
box 5                                   G/14   E/17   ← shares G/14

Pull out the chain and you get a single path of As climbing the neck, one per box:

E/5  →  D/7  →  B/10  →  A/12  →  G/14  →  E/17
 ↑       ↑       ↑        ↑        ↑        ↑
box 1   box 2   box 3    box 4    box 5   box 1 again (octave up)

The drill: play only those six notes, in order, up and back. Six notes, all A, spanning the whole neck.

Why this matters: it's a navigation spine. Instead of remembering "which box is which," you find the nearest root on the spine and the box assembles around it. Boxes become consequences of a root position rather than five things to recall.

Test: play the spine up and back without hesitation. Then land on any spine note and play the box that surrounds it.

Drill 4.3 · The stretch in box 3​

Box 3 is the awkward one, and it's worth knowing exactly why rather than assuming your hand is wrong. Two strings have three-fret gaps:

   G string:   9 (E)  ─────►  12 (G)      three frets
   B string:  10 (A)  ─────►  13 (C)      three frets

Every other box has gaps of two. These two are the stretch.

The drill: isolate just those two moves. Play G/9 → G/12 and B/10 → B/13 slowly, alternating fingers 1 and 4, with the thumb low behind the neck rather than hooked over the top. Do not force it — if it hurts, stop.

Test: both stretches, clean, ten times, without the hand tensing.

Two honest notes. Reaching this comfortably takes weeks, not days, and hand size genuinely matters — a smaller hand may prefer to shift slightly rather than stretch, which is a legitimate solution rather than cheating. And per file 08, pain is a stop signal, not a discipline problem.

The boxes are not in this picture. They're five windows somebody chose to draw on it. The pattern is continuous, and once you can see that, "which box am I in" stops being a question you need to answer.

The drill: ascend from fret 2 to fret 17 through all five boxes and back down, in time, slowly.

Test: the whole neck, up and back, with no pause at any seam.

Drill 4.5 · The twelve-fret repeat​

Look at the map again and compare fret 5 with fret 17: identical. Same for fret 2 and fret 14.

E/5 = A          E/17 = A
G/2 = A          G/14 = A

Everything repeats after twelve frets, because that's an octave. So the map above is really only twelve frets of information, printed one and a half times.

The drill: play box 1 at fret 5, then the same shape at fret 17, and hear that it's the same thing an octave up.

Test: name any fret above 12 and immediately give its equivalent below 12.

Drill 4.6 · Graduation​

The test for whether this whole set worked:

Name any note in the scale, and find every instance of it on the neck in under thirty seconds, without counting up from an open string.

Do it for all five: A, C, D, E, G. When that's comfortable, you know the fretboard for this scale — which is what these four pages were for.

At that point the geography is done, and the useful question changes from "where are the notes?" to "which ones should I play, and when?" That's a different subject, and it's the one in Stage 2 · Making Music With It.

Takeaway​

  • Learn the four remaining boxes one per session, roots first — they interfere with each other if crammed.
  • The root spine: E/5 → D/7 → B/10 → A/12 → G/14 → E/17. Consecutive boxes share a root, so the boxes hang off one climbing path of As.
  • Box 3's stretch is real — three-fret gaps on G/9→12 and B/10→13. Weeks, not days; shifting instead of stretching is legitimate; pain means stop.
  • The full-neck map shows the boxes don't exist — they're five chosen windows on one continuous pattern.
  • Everything repeats after twelve frets: it's twelve frets of information, not seventeen.
  • Graduation: find every instance of any scale note in under thirty seconds.

That completes the fretboard set. The next question — which notes and when — is a different skill: Stage 2 · Making Music With It.
