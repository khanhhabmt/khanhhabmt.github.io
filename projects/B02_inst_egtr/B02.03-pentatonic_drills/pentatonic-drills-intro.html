🗺️ Stage 1 · Pentatonic Drills

These pages are the practical companion to Pentatonic First. That file explains what the pentatonic is and why the path runs the way it does. These pages are the actual exercises, with a diagram for each one.

Scope, stated up front: this set is about knowing the fretboard. Not phrasing, not vocabulary, not soloing over changes. The goal is narrow and worth being narrow about:

By the end you should be able to find any pentatonic note, in any position, and know its name and its distance from the root — without counting.

Musicianship is deliberately not mixed in here. Learning the alphabet before words is a legitimate order; it just has to be followed by words — and the first of those pages is Stage 2 · Making Music With It, which assumes this set is already done. If finding a note still takes you a moment of searching, you're in the right place; if it doesn't, go there.

Everything is in A minor pentatonic​

One key throughout: A C D E G, boxes anchored at fret 5. This is simultaneously C major pentatonic — see the major/minor section — and every shape here transposes to any key by sliding.

Note one convenience: A minor pentatonic contains no accidentals, so every note name in these diagrams is a single letter. Nothing to mistype, nothing to misread.

How to read the diagrams​

●   root note (A)              ◆   fret marker (the neck's inlay dots)
○   note in the scale          ┼   fret wire

High E is the top line, low E the bottom, matching tab. Fret numbers underneath. Two grid widths are used: wide cells for single-box diagrams, narrow cells for full-neck views.

How to use these pages​

This is a library, not a checklist. There are more exercises here than belong in any one session. The rule from A Routine You Will Keep still governs: a 10-minute session that happens beats a 60-minute session that doesn't. Take one or two drills per session and rotate.

Order matters more than volume, though. The pages are sequenced, and each assumes the one before:

PageWhat it builds
01 · Box Oneone shape, and where its roots are
02 · Going Horizontalthe neck along its length — before box-thinking hardens
03 · Connectingthe seams between adjacent boxes
04 · The Whole Neckthe remaining boxes, and the map they sit on

The one sequencing rule worth obeying strictly: do page 02 before page 04. Learning all five boxes before you can move along the neck is how players end up in box prison — five shapes, no ability to travel between them.
