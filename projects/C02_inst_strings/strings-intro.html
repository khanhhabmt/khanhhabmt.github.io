🎸 String Instruments

Music theory is written for ideal strings — infinitely thin, perfectly flexible, tuned to clean ratios. Real strings are none of those things. They're stiff, thick, under enormous tension, and glued to a wooden box that moves with the weather. This section lives in the gap between the two.

The organizing question:

What happens when the tidy math of tuning meets an actual vibrating string?

It turns out that every real string instrument is a stack of compromises, and the compromises are visible if you know where to look — in the slanted saddle of an acoustic guitar, in the way a piano's top octave is tuned deliberately sharp, in the "squiggly" frets some players install. None of these are mistakes. They're the instrument paying the bill that equal temperament and ideal-string theory quietly left on the table.

Why piano and guitar belong together here​

Both are string instruments — a piano is just struck strings, a guitar plucked ones — and both are forced into the same two reckonings:

  1. Temperament — you cannot have pure intervals and a keyboard/fretboard that plays in every key. Something has to be detuned on purpose.
  2. Inharmonicity — real stiff strings produce overtones that aren't exact whole-number multiples, so even a "mathematically correct" tuning sounds wrong until you bend it to fit the actual string.

What's here now​

Five subfolders, roughly ordered physics → temperament → theory-on-the-instrument → the player:

1. tuning/ — Tuning & Temperament. Why pure 3:2 fifths can't close the circle, the spectrum from just intonation to equal temperament, piano stretch tuning, and how 2^(n/12) gets carved into a guitar fretboard. (Builds on the derivation in the Circle of Fifths section.)

2. acoustics/ — How a String Makes Sound. The vibrating-string equation and what controls pitch, why where you pluck changes the tone, and how a nearly silent string gets turned into sound by a body or a pickup.

3. fretless_and_just_intonation/ — The Other Side of Temperament. Take the frets off and pitch becomes continuous — so violinists and singers can play pure just intonation that a fretted, equal-tempered guitar never can. This is the mirror image of the tuning story.

4. fretboard_as_a_map/ — The Neck as a Coordinate System. Why the fretboard lets you slide shapes around, why standard tuning is the odd EADGBE, and how the circle of fifths is literally sitting across the strings.

5. mindsets/ — Guitarist vs Pianist vs Both. The layout doesn't just shape the instrument, it shapes the player: why guitarists think in movable shapes and pianists in named notes, what each model is blind to, and how to run both.

This is a living section — technique and setup notes will land alongside these over time.
