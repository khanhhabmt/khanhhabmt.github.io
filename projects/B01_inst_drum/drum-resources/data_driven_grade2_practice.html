Data-Driven Practice for Trinity Grade 2 Drums

Measuring What Matters: A Quantitative Approach to Exam Preparation

Using Roland TD-17, Mac M1, and Python to track, analyze, and optimize your practice over 12 weeks.

Why Data-Driven Practice?​

You have 3 months. Three performance pieces. Technical exercises. Supporting tests. And one shot at the exam.

That's 12 weeks. Roughly 48 practice sessions if you're disciplined. Every session counts.

Traditional practice advice: "Just play it every day until it feels good."

The problem: "Feels good" is subjective, unreliable, and often wrong.

Your brain adapts to your current level. What felt "tight" last week sounds sloppy this week. What felt "fast enough" in your bedroom falls apart under exam pressure. Without objective measurement, you're flying blind.

This is where electronic drums become a superpower.

The Roland TD-17 doesn't just make sound—it generates data. Every hit you play is recorded as MIDI: exact timing (down to the millisecond), exact velocity (how hard you hit), exact duration. This data can be extracted, analyzed, and visualized to answer questions like:

  • Am I actually improving, or just getting comfortable with mediocrity?
  • Which specific bars in Piece #2 am I rushing?
  • Is my left hand weaker than my right, and by how much?
  • Can I maintain precision for the full 15-minute exam duration?

This guide shows you how to turn your practice sessions into a feedback loop: measure → analyze → adjust → improve.

The Four Dimensions of Exam Performance​

Adapted from the "Four Dimensions" framework, here's what Trinity Grade 2 examiners are actually listening for:

Dimension 1: Timing Precision (The Grid)​

What it is: How consistently you hit notes relative to the beat.

Why it matters: Examiners notice rushing, dragging, and tempo drift immediately. Even if you play all the right notes, poor timing = lower marks.

Measurable metrics:

  • Mean deviation from grid (milliseconds)
  • Standard deviation (consistency)
  • Tempo drift over time (BPM change)

Dimension 2: Dynamic Control (Velocity)​

What it is: Your ability to control volume—soft vs. loud, accents vs. ghost notes.

Why it matters: Grade 2 pieces require dynamic contrast. Playing everything at the same volume sounds robotic and unmusical.

Measurable metrics:

  • Velocity range (min/max MIDI values)
  • Velocity consistency (standard deviation)
  • Accent clarity (ratio of accented to unaccented hits)

Dimension 3: Technical Consistency (Rudiments & Patterns)​

What it is: Clean execution of technical exercises (paradiddles, rolls, flams, etc.)

Why it matters: Technical work accounts for 14-22% of your exam marks. Sloppy rudiments = lost points.

Measurable metrics:

  • Error rate (missed or incorrect strokes)
  • Tempo ceiling (max BPM before breakdown)
  • Stroke evenness (velocity consistency within rudiments)

Dimension 4: Stamina & Endurance (Performance Degradation)​

What it is: How well you maintain quality over the full exam duration (~15 minutes).

Why it matters: You might nail Piece #1, but if you're exhausted by Piece #3, your marks suffer.

Measurable metrics:

  • Timing precision degradation (first 2 min vs. last 2 min)
  • Velocity drop-off
  • Error frequency increase

Setup: Roland TD-17 + Mac M1​

Step 1: Connect TD-17 to Mac via USB​

  1. Cable: Use a standard USB 2.0 cable (Type-B to Type-A or Type-C adapter for M1 Macs)
  2. Driver: Download the Roland USB driver from roland.com/support
    • For macOS 10.13+, you may need to approve the driver in System Settings → Privacy & Security
  3. TD-17 Settings:
    • Navigate to: SETUP → USB → USB Driver Mode
    • Set to: VENDOR
    • Power off and on for changes to take effect
  4. Enable MIDI Transmission:
    • Navigate to: SETUP → MIDI → MIDI Tx/Rx
    • Set to: ON

Step 2: Choose Your DAW (Digital Audio Workstation)​

You need software to record MIDI data. Options for Mac M1:

DAWCostProsCons
GarageBandFreePre-installed, simpleLimited MIDI editing
Logic Pro$200Professional, powerful MIDI editorLearning curve
Reaper$60Lightweight, customizableLess polished UI

Recommendation for beginners: Start with GarageBand. Upgrade to Logic Pro if you need advanced analysis.

Step 3: Configure DAW to Receive MIDI​

In GarageBand:

  1. Create a new project → Empty Project
  2. Add a Software Instrument track
  3. In track settings, set MIDI Input to: TD-17
  4. Arm the track for recording (red button)
  5. Hit a pad → You should see MIDI activity

In Logic Pro:

  1. Create new project → Empty Project → Software Instrument
  2. Logic Pro → Settings → MIDI → Inputs
  3. Enable TD-17 in the device list
  4. Arm track, test MIDI input

Measurement Protocols​

Protocol 1: Timing Precision Analysis​

Goal: Measure how accurately you're hitting the beat.

Workflow:

  1. Record yourself playing a piece with a click track
  2. Export MIDI file from DAW
  3. Run Python script to calculate timing deviations
  4. Generate report + visualization

Python Script: analyze_timing.py

import mido
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def analyze_timing(midi_file, expected_bpm):
    """
    Analyzes timing precision of a MIDI drum recording.
    
    Args:
        midi_file: Path to MIDI file
        expected_bpm: The tempo you were playing to (e.g., 120)
    
    Returns:
        Dictionary with timing statistics
    """
    mid = mido.MidiFile(midi_file)
    
    # Extract note onset times (in seconds)
    onset_times = []
    current_time = 0
    
    for msg in mid:
        current_time += msg.time
        if msg.type == 'note_on' and msg.velocity > 0:
            onset_times.append(current_time)
    
    onset_times = np.array(onset_times)
    
    # Calculate expected beat times based on BPM
    beat_interval = 60.0 / expected_bpm  # seconds per beat
    expected_beats = np.arange(0, onset_times[-1], beat_interval)
    
    # Calculate deviation of each note from nearest beat
    deviations_ms = []
    for onset in onset_times:
        nearest_beat = expected_beats[np.abs(expected_beats - onset).argmin()]
        deviation = (onset - nearest_beat) * 1000  # convert to ms
        deviations_ms.append(deviation)
    
    deviations_ms = np.array(deviations_ms)
    
    # Calculate statistics
    stats = {
        'mean_deviation_ms': np.mean(deviations_ms),
        'std_deviation_ms': np.std(deviations_ms),
        'max_deviation_ms': np.max(np.abs(deviations_ms)),
        'total_notes': len(onset_times)
    }
    
    # Visualize
    plt.figure(figsize=(12, 5))
    plt.hist(deviations_ms, bins=50, color='steelblue', edgecolor='black', alpha=0.7)
    plt.axvline(0, color='red', linestyle='--', linewidth=2, label='Perfect Grid')
    plt.xlabel('Deviation from Beat (ms)', fontsize=12)
    plt.ylabel('Frequency', fontsize=12)
    plt.title(f'Timing Precision Analysis\nMean: {stats["mean_deviation_ms"]:.2f}ms | Std Dev: {stats["std_deviation_ms"]:.2f}ms', fontsize=14)
    plt.legend()
    plt.grid(alpha=0.3)
    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.savefig('timing_analysis.png', dpi=150)
    plt.show()
    
    return stats

# Example usage
if __name__ == "__main__":
    stats = analyze_timing("Week_01/Piece_A1_Take1.mid", expected_bpm=120)
    
    print("\n=== TIMING PRECISION REPORT ===")
    print(f"Total Notes: {stats['total_notes']}")
    print(f"Mean Deviation: {stats['mean_deviation_ms']:.2f} ms")
    print(f"Std Deviation: {stats['std_deviation_ms']:.2f} ms")
    print(f"Max Deviation: {stats['max_deviation_ms']:.2f} ms")
    
    # Interpretation
    if stats['std_deviation_ms'] < 15:
        print("\n✓ Assessment: Excellent precision. Exam-ready timing.")
    elif stats['std_deviation_ms'] < 30:
        print("\n⚠ Assessment: Good, but room for improvement.")
    else:
        print("\n✗ Assessment: Needs work. Focus on slow practice with click.")

What to track weekly:

  • Standard deviation trend (should decrease over time)
  • Mean deviation (should approach 0)

Target for Grade 2:

  • Std deviation < 20ms (good)
  • Std deviation < 15ms (excellent)

Protocol 2: Dynamic Control Analysis​

Goal: Measure your ability to control volume and dynamics.

Workflow:

  1. Record a piece that requires dynamic contrast (e.g., soft verse, loud chorus)
  2. Export MIDI file
  3. Analyze velocity data

Python Script: analyze_velocity.py

import mido
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def analyze_velocity(midi_file, drum_note=38):
    """
    Analyzes velocity (dynamics) of a specific drum.
    
    Args:
        midi_file: Path to MIDI file
        drum_note: MIDI note number (38 = snare, 36 = kick, 42 = hi-hat closed)
    
    Returns:
        Dictionary with velocity statistics
    """
    mid = mido.MidiFile(midi_file)
    
    # Extract velocities for the specified drum
    velocities = []
    times = []
    current_time = 0
    
    for msg in mid:
        current_time += msg.time
        if msg.type == 'note_on' and msg.note == drum_note and msg.velocity > 0:
            velocities.append(msg.velocity)
            times.append(current_time)
    
    velocities = np.array(velocities)
    times = np.array(times)
    
    # Calculate statistics
    stats = {
        'mean_velocity': np.mean(velocities),
        'std_velocity': np.std(velocities),
        'min_velocity': np.min(velocities),
        'max_velocity': np.max(velocities),
        'dynamic_range': np.max(velocities) - np.min(velocities),
        'total_hits': len(velocities)
    }
    
    # Visualize velocity over time
    fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(14, 8))
    
    # Velocity over time
    ax1.plot(times, velocities, marker='o', linestyle='-', alpha=0.6, markersize=4)
    ax1.axhline(stats['mean_velocity'], color='red', linestyle='--', label=f'Mean: {stats["mean_velocity"]:.1f}')
    ax1.set_xlabel('Time (s)', fontsize=12)
    ax1.set_ylabel('Velocity (MIDI 0-127)', fontsize=12)
    ax1.set_title('Velocity Over Time', fontsize=14)
    ax1.legend()
    ax1.grid(alpha=0.3)
    
    # Velocity histogram
    ax2.hist(velocities, bins=30, color='purple', edgecolor='black', alpha=0.7)
    ax2.axvline(stats['mean_velocity'], color='red', linestyle='--', linewidth=2, label='Mean')
    ax2.set_xlabel('Velocity (MIDI 0-127)', fontsize=12)
    ax2.set_ylabel('Frequency', fontsize=12)
    ax2.set_title('Velocity Distribution', fontsize=14)
    ax2.legend()
    ax2.grid(alpha=0.3)
    
    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.savefig('velocity_analysis.png', dpi=150)
    plt.show()
    
    return stats

# Example usage
if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Analyze snare drum (MIDI note 38)
    stats = analyze_velocity("Week_01/Piece_A1_Take1.mid", drum_note=38)
    
    print("\n=== DYNAMIC CONTROL REPORT (Snare) ===")
    print(f"Total Hits: {stats['total_hits']}")
    print(f"Mean Velocity: {stats['mean_velocity']:.1f}")
    print(f"Std Deviation: {stats['std_velocity']:.1f}")
    print(f"Dynamic Range: {stats['min_velocity']} - {stats['max_velocity']} (Range: {stats['dynamic_range']})")
    
    # Interpretation
    if stats['dynamic_range'] < 20:
        print("\n✗ Assessment: Flat dynamics. Work on ghost notes and accents.")
    elif stats['dynamic_range'] < 40:
        print("\n⚠ Assessment: Moderate dynamics. Good, but could be more expressive.")
    else:
        print("\n✓ Assessment: Excellent dynamic range. Expressive playing.")

What to track weekly:

  • Dynamic range (should increase as you gain control)
  • Consistency of ghost notes vs. accents

Target for Grade 2:

  • Dynamic range > 40 (good contrast between soft and loud)
  • Clear separation between ghost notes (<60) and accents (>90)

Protocol 3: Technical Exercise Tracking​

Goal: Measure progress on the 3 technical exercises in your exam book.

Workflow:

  1. Record each technical exercise at your current max tempo
  2. Track tempo ceiling weekly
  3. Analyze error rate (missed strokes)

Python Script: analyze_technical.py

import mido
import numpy as np

def analyze_technical_exercise(midi_file, expected_bpm, expected_pattern_length):
    """
    Analyzes technical exercise execution.
    
    Args:
        midi_file: Path to MIDI file
        expected_bpm: Tempo you were playing at
        expected_pattern_length: Number of notes in one repetition of the pattern
    
    Returns:
        Dictionary with technical statistics
    """
    mid = mido.MidiFile(midi_file)
    
    # Extract note onset times and velocities
    onset_times = []
    velocities = []
    current_time = 0
    
    for msg in mid:
        current_time += msg.time
        if msg.type == 'note_on' and msg.velocity > 0:
            onset_times.append(current_time)
            velocities.append(msg.velocity)
    
    onset_times = np.array(onset_times)
    velocities = np.array(velocities)
    
    # Calculate inter-onset intervals (time between hits)
    intervals = np.diff(onset_times)
    expected_interval = 60.0 / expected_bpm  # seconds per beat
    
    # Detect errors (intervals significantly different from expected)
    error_threshold = expected_interval * 0.3  # 30% deviation = error
    errors = np.abs(intervals - expected_interval) > error_threshold
    error_rate = np.sum(errors) / len(intervals) * 100
    
    # Calculate tempo stability (BPM drift)
    # Split into first half and second half
    mid_point = len(intervals) // 2
    first_half_intervals = intervals[:mid_point]
    second_half_intervals = intervals[mid_point:]
    
    first_half_bpm = 60.0 / np.mean(first_half_intervals)
    second_half_bpm = 60.0 / np.mean(second_half_intervals)
    tempo_drift = second_half_bpm - first_half_bpm
    
    # Calculate stroke evenness (velocity consistency)
    velocity_std = np.std(velocities)
    
    stats = {
        'total_notes': len(onset_times),
        'error_rate_percent': error_rate,
        'tempo_drift_bpm': tempo_drift,
        'velocity_std': velocity_std,
        'mean_velocity': np.mean(velocities)
    }
    
    return stats

# Example usage
if __name__ == "__main__":
    stats = analyze_technical_exercise(
        "Week_01/Technical_Ex1.mid",
        expected_bpm=100,
        expected_pattern_length=16
    )
    
    print("\n=== TECHNICAL EXERCISE REPORT ===")
    print(f"Total Notes: {stats['total_notes']}")
    print(f"Error Rate: {stats['error_rate_percent']:.1f}%")
    print(f"Tempo Drift: {stats['tempo_drift_bpm']:+.2f} BPM")
    print(f"Stroke Evenness (Velocity Std): {stats['velocity_std']:.1f}")
    
    # Interpretation
    if stats['error_rate_percent'] < 5:
        print("\n✓ Assessment: Clean execution. Ready to increase tempo.")
    elif stats['error_rate_percent'] < 10:
        print("\n⚠ Assessment: Mostly clean, but some errors. Slow down slightly.")
    else:
        print("\n✗ Assessment: Too many errors. Reduce tempo and focus on accuracy.")

What to track weekly:

  • Tempo ceiling (max BPM before error rate > 10%)
  • Error rate at target tempo

Target for Grade 2:

  • Error rate < 5% at exam tempo
  • Tempo drift < ±3 BPM

Protocol 4: Stamina & Endurance Analysis​

Goal: Ensure you can maintain quality for the full exam duration.

Workflow:

  1. Record a full mock exam (~15 minutes: 3 pieces + technical work)
  2. Analyze timing precision and velocity in 3-minute segments
  3. Measure degradation over time

Python Script: analyze_stamina.py

import mido
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def analyze_stamina(midi_file, expected_bpm, segment_duration=180):
    """
    Analyzes performance degradation over time.
    
    Args:
        midi_file: Path to MIDI file of full mock exam
        expected_bpm: Average tempo
        segment_duration: Length of each analysis segment (seconds)
    
    Returns:
        Dictionary with stamina statistics
    """
    mid = mido.MidiFile(midi_file)
    
    # Extract note data
    onset_times = []
    velocities = []
    current_time = 0
    
    for msg in mid:
        current_time += msg.time
        if msg.type == 'note_on' and msg.velocity > 0:
            onset_times.append(current_time)
            velocities.append(msg.velocity)
    
    onset_times = np.array(onset_times)
    velocities = np.array(velocities)
    
    # Split into segments
    total_duration = onset_times[-1]
    num_segments = int(np.ceil(total_duration / segment_duration))
    
    segment_stats = []
    
    for i in range(num_segments):
        start_time = i * segment_duration
        end_time = (i + 1) * segment_duration
        
        # Get notes in this segment
        mask = (onset_times >= start_time) & (onset_times < end_time)
        segment_onsets = onset_times[mask]
        segment_velocities = velocities[mask]
        
        if len(segment_onsets) < 10:  # Skip if too few notes
            continue
        
        # Calculate timing precision for this segment
        beat_interval = 60.0 / expected_bpm
        expected_beats = np.arange(start_time, end_time, beat_interval)
        
        deviations = []
        for onset in segment_onsets:
            nearest_beat = expected_beats[np.abs(expected_beats - onset).argmin()]
            deviation = (onset - nearest_beat) * 1000
            deviations.append(deviation)
        
        timing_std = np.std(deviations)
        velocity_mean = np.mean(segment_velocities)
        
        segment_stats.append({
            'segment': i + 1,
            'timing_std_ms': timing_std,
            'velocity_mean': velocity_mean
        })
    
    # Calculate degradation
    first_segment = segment_stats[0]
    last_segment = segment_stats[-1]
    
    timing_degradation = last_segment['timing_std_ms'] - first_segment['timing_std_ms']
    velocity_degradation = first_segment['velocity_mean'] - last_segment['velocity_mean']
    
    # Visualize
    segments = [s['segment'] for s in segment_stats]
    timing_stds = [s['timing_std_ms'] for s in segment_stats]
    velocity_means = [s['velocity_mean'] for s in segment_stats]
    
    fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(12, 8))
    
    # Timing precision over segments
    ax1.plot(segments, timing_stds, marker='o', linewidth=2, markersize=8, color='steelblue')
    ax1.set_xlabel('Segment (3-min intervals)', fontsize=12)
    ax1.set_ylabel('Timing Std Dev (ms)', fontsize=12)
    ax1.set_title('Timing Precision Over Time (Lower = Better)', fontsize=14)
    ax1.grid(alpha=0.3)
    
    # Velocity over segments
    ax2.plot(segments, velocity_means, marker='o', linewidth=2, markersize=8, color='purple')
    ax2.set_xlabel('Segment (3-min intervals)', fontsize=12)
    ax2.set_ylabel('Mean Velocity', fontsize=12)
    ax2.set_title('Dynamic Energy Over Time', fontsize=14)
    ax2.grid(alpha=0.3)
    
    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.savefig('stamina_analysis.png', dpi=150)
    plt.show()
    
    return {
        'timing_degradation_ms': timing_degradation,
        'velocity_degradation': velocity_degradation,
        'segment_stats': segment_stats
    }

# Example usage
if __name__ == "__main__":
    stats = analyze_stamina("Week_01/Full_Mock_Exam.mid", expected_bpm=120)
    
    print("\n=== STAMINA & ENDURANCE REPORT ===")
    print(f"Timing Degradation: {stats['timing_degradation_ms']:+.2f} ms")
    print(f"Velocity Degradation: {stats['velocity_degradation']:+.1f}")
    
    # Interpretation
    if stats['timing_degradation_ms'] < 5:
        print("\n✓ Assessment: Excellent stamina. No significant degradation.")
    elif stats['timing_degradation_ms'] < 10:
        print("\n⚠ Assessment: Slight degradation. Work on endurance.")
    else:
        print("\n✗ Assessment: Significant fatigue. Build stamina with longer practice sessions.")

What to track weekly:

  • Timing degradation (should decrease as stamina improves)
  • Velocity degradation (should stay minimal)

Target for Grade 2:

  • Timing degradation < 5ms
  • Velocity degradation < 10 points

The 12-Week Phased Timeline​

With only 12 weeks, you can't afford to spend the first 4 weeks "just getting comfortable." Every phase has a specific purpose.

Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-4) — "Chẩn đoán & Xây nền"​

Goal: Establish baseline measurements, identify weaknesses, build correct motor patterns.

DayFocusAction
MondayTiming BaselineRecord all 3 pieces with click. Run analyze_timing.py. Note weak bars.
WednesdayTechnical BaselineRecord 3 technical exercises. Find current tempo ceiling.
FridayDynamics & PiecesRecord pieces focusing on dynamic contrast. Run analyze_velocity.py.
SundayData ReviewGenerate Week N dashboard. Plan next week's focus.

Phase 1 Targets:

  • Identify the 3-5 bars where timing deviation is worst
  • Establish tempo ceiling for each technical exercise
  • Document left-hand vs. right-hand velocity gap

Key Rule: Do NOT increase tempo in Phase 1. Accuracy first, speed later.

Phase 2: Build (Weeks 5-8) — "Tăng tốc & Sửa lỗi"​

Goal: Systematically eliminate weaknesses, increase tempo ceilings, build endurance.

DayFocusAction
MondayProblem Bar SurgeryIsolate and loop the 3-5 weakest bars. Slow practice → record → analyze.
WednesdayTempo Push+5 BPM on technical exercises if error rate < 5%.
FridayFull Mock ExamRecord full 15-min simulation. Run analyze_stamina.py.
SundayData Review + VideoReview dashboard + watch video recording of Friday's mock.

Phase 2 Targets:

  • Timing std dev < 20ms on all pieces
  • Technical exercises at 90% of exam tempo
  • Complete a full mock exam without stopping

Phase 3: Polish (Weeks 9-12) — "Đánh bóng & Mô phỏng thi"​

Goal: Exam simulation, confidence building, fine-tuning musicality.

DayFocusAction
MondayExam SimulationFull mock exam under exam conditions (see Exam Day Simulation below).
WednesdayWeakest LinkFocus exclusively on lowest-scoring dimension from data.
FridaySecond Mock ExamAnother full simulation. Compare to Monday's data.
SundayRest + Light ReviewLight review of data. Mental preparation. Do NOT over-practice.

Phase 3 Targets:

  • Timing std dev < 15ms (excellent)
  • Dynamic range > 40
  • Error rate < 5% at exam tempo
  • Stamina degradation < 5ms
  • Readiness Score > 80%

Third-Party Tools: Beyond DIY Python​

Python scripts give you maximum flexibility, but you don't always need to write code. Here are tools that provide instant feedback with zero programming:

Melodics (Free tier available)​

  • What: Interactive drum lesson app with real-time MIDI analysis
  • Setup: Connect TD-17 via USB → Melodics auto-detects
  • Data it provides: Timing accuracy per hit (early/late), accuracy percentage, streak tracking
  • Best for: Daily warm-up with gamified feedback. The "accuracy %" gives you a quick daily health check without running scripts
  • Limitation: Lessons are pre-set, can't analyze your exam pieces

Moises (Free tier available)​

  • What: AI-powered audio separation tool (stem splitter)
  • Setup: Upload any song → Moises separates drums, bass, vocals, etc.
  • Data it provides: Drum-less backing tracks for your exam pieces
  • Best for: Practicing your exam pieces with professional-quality backing tracks minus the original drums. Also useful for analyzing professional drummers' parts in isolation

LiveBPM (Free, Android/iOS)​

  • What: Real-time tempo detection via microphone
  • Setup: Place phone near kit → LiveBPM listens and displays live BPM
  • Data it provides: Real-time BPM readout, tempo drift visualization
  • Best for: Quick check of tempo stability during clickless practice. No MIDI required—works with acoustic kits too

TD-17 Built-in Coach Mode​

  • What: The TD-17 module itself has a built-in practice coach
  • Setup: MENU → COACH → Time Check or Quiet Count
  • Data it provides: On-screen timing accuracy feedback
  • Best for: Zero-setup timing practice when you don't want to boot up the Mac

Protocol 5: Video Self-Assessment​

MIDI data tells you what happened musically. Video tells you why it happened physically.

Setup:

  • Phone on tripod, angled to see both hands, both feet, and your posture
  • Frame so the drummer fills at least 60% of the screen
  • Record simultaneously with MIDI (start DAW recording, then hit record on phone)

What to look for:

Problem (from MIDI data)Visual Clue (from video)
Left hand consistently softerLeft wrist not lifting as high, or death-gripping the stick
Rushing fillsShoulders tensing up before the fill, leaning forward
Kick timing unstableHeel lifting off the pedal, ankle locked, using full leg
Stamina degradationPosture collapsing, elbows drifting outward, jaw clenching
Inconsistent hi-hatWrist angle changing, fulcrum point shifting on the stick

Weekly Video Ritual:

  1. Record Friday's mock exam on video
  2. Watch it on Sunday at 0.5x speed
  3. Note 1-2 physical habits to fix next week
  4. Compare video from Week N and Week N-4 to see physical improvement

Exam-Specific Benefit: If you're doing a digital/video exam submission, this practice doubles as preparation for performing on camera. You get used to the pressure of "being recorded."

Protocol 6: Left-Right Hand Imbalance Analysis​

One of the most common issues at Grade 2 level: your dominant hand is significantly stronger and more accurate than your weak hand, but you don't realize it until the data shows you.

Python Script: analyze_hand_balance.py

import mido
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def analyze_hand_balance(midi_file, left_note=38, right_note=48):
    """
    Compares velocity and timing between left and right hand hits.
    
    For TD-17 default mapping:
    - Note 38 (Snare center) = typically hit by left hand in standard setup
    - Note 48 (Tom 1) or Note 46 (Hi-hat open) = typically right hand
    
    Adjust note numbers based on your pad assignment and sticking.
    """
    mid = mido.MidiFile(midi_file)
    
    left_velocities = []
    right_velocities = []
    left_times = []
    right_times = []
    current_time = 0
    
    for msg in mid:
        current_time += msg.time
        if msg.type == 'note_on' and msg.velocity > 0:
            if msg.note == left_note:
                left_velocities.append(msg.velocity)
                left_times.append(current_time)
            elif msg.note == right_note:
                right_velocities.append(msg.velocity)
                right_times.append(current_time)
    
    left_vel = np.array(left_velocities)
    right_vel = np.array(right_velocities)
    
    # Calculate imbalance
    left_mean = np.mean(left_vel) if len(left_vel) > 0 else 0
    right_mean = np.mean(right_vel) if len(right_vel) > 0 else 0
    imbalance = abs(right_mean - left_mean)
    
    print("\n=== HAND BALANCE REPORT ===")
    print(f"Left Hand (note {left_note}):  Mean Velocity = {left_mean:.1f} | Hits = {len(left_vel)}")
    print(f"Right Hand (note {right_note}): Mean Velocity = {right_mean:.1f} | Hits = {len(right_vel)}")
    print(f"Imbalance: {imbalance:.1f} points")
    
    if imbalance < 5:
        print("✓ Assessment: Excellent balance. Hands are even.")
    elif imbalance < 15:
        print("⚠ Assessment: Noticeable imbalance. Weak hand needs focused work.")
    else:
        print("✗ Assessment: Significant imbalance. Prioritize weak hand exercises.")
    
    # Visualize
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 5))
    ax.hist(left_vel, bins=20, alpha=0.6, label=f'Left (mean: {left_mean:.1f})', color='steelblue')
    ax.hist(right_vel, bins=20, alpha=0.6, label=f'Right (mean: {right_mean:.1f})', color='coral')
    ax.set_xlabel('Velocity (MIDI 0-127)', fontsize=12)
    ax.set_ylabel('Frequency', fontsize=12)
    ax.set_title('Left vs Right Hand Velocity Distribution', fontsize=14)
    ax.legend()
    ax.grid(alpha=0.3)
    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.savefig('hand_balance.png', dpi=150)
    plt.show()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    analyze_hand_balance("Week_01/Technical_Ex1.mid")

  • Which hand has more timing errors

Target for Grade 2:

  • Velocity imbalance < 5 points (excellent)
  • Velocity imbalance < 10 points (acceptable)

Exam Day Simulation Protocol​

Starting from Phase 3 (Week 9), simulate real exam conditions at least twice a week:

The Setup​

  1. No warm-up pieces — In the real exam, you walk in and play. Practice this.
  2. Play in order — Piece A1 → Piece A2 → Piece B (solo) → Technical Exercises. No breaks, no restarts.
  3. One take only — No "let me start over." In the exam, mistakes stay.
  4. Record everything — MIDI + video + audio (phone mic is fine for audio reference).
  5. Use backing tracks — Group A pieces must be played with backing tracks in the exam.

The Scorecard​

After each simulation, score yourself honestly:

CriterionScore (1-5)Notes
Timing consistencyDid I maintain pulse throughout?
Dynamic contrastWere soft parts soft and loud parts loud?
Technical accuracyAny missed strokes or wrong sticking?
Recovery from mistakesDid I recover gracefully or freeze?
Physical comfortWas I relaxed or tense at the end?
Musical expressionDid it sound like music, or like an exercise?
TOTAL/30

Target: Score consistently > 24/30 before exam day.

The Pressure Injection​

To simulate exam nerves:

  • Record for someone else to watch. Tell a friend/teacher you'll send them the video.
  • Set a timer. Give yourself 20 minutes total, including setup. Time pressure mimics exam reality.
  • Play on a different kit if possible — or at least rearrange your setup slightly. Exam rooms are unfamiliar.

Psychological Integration: Data + Inner Clock​

The Paradox of Measurement:

Data is powerful, but it can also create anxiety. Obsessing over milliseconds can make you less musical, not more.

The key is balance.

Use Data to Build Confidence, Not Destroy It​

Good use of data:

  • "My timing std dev dropped from 28ms to 15ms in 8 weeks. I'm improving."
  • "My left hand velocity is 10 points lower than my right. I'll focus on that."

Bad use of data:

  • "I was 3ms off on bar 12. I'm terrible."
  • "My velocity std dev is 12.7 instead of 12.5. I'm getting worse."

Rule: Only compare week-to-week trends, not individual takes.

Data Calibrates the Inner Clock​

Remember the "Inner Clock" framework: the goal is to generate time internally, not react to external cues.

Data helps you calibrate that internal sense:

  1. Practice without click (trust your inner clock)
  2. Record the take (capture what your inner clock produced)
  3. Analyze the data (see how accurate your inner clock was)
  4. Adjust (if you rushed, practice feeling "behind"; if you dragged, practice feeling "ahead")

This feedback loop trains your basal ganglia to generate more accurate time.

When to Ignore the Data​

During performance:

  • Don't think about milliseconds
  • Don't calculate velocity
  • Just play

During creative practice:

  • Experimenting with feel
  • Exploring new grooves
  • Playing for fun

Data is a tool for deliberate practice, not for performance or exploration.

Conclusion: The Exam-Ready Drummer​

By June 2026, you will have:

  • 48 practice sessions worth of data
  • 144 MIDI recordings (3 pieces × 12 weeks × 4 sessions/week)
  • 12 weeks of progress visualized in dashboards

You'll know, with certainty:

  • Which bars you rush
  • Which hand is weaker
  • Whether you can maintain quality for 15 minutes
  • How close you are to exam-ready standards

This is not guesswork. This is measurement.

And when you walk into that exam room, you won't be hoping you're ready.

You'll know you're ready.

Because the data says so.
