Beyond Twelve

Stay inside the Western frame — harmonic instruments, the fifth as generator, an octave divided into equal steps — and twelve still isn't forced. Several other divisions are measurably better, were discovered centuries ago, were built into real instruments, and lost anyway.

Understanding why they lost is more useful than understanding why twelve won.

1. How to score a division​

Take n equal steps per octave (written n-EDO, equal division of the octave). Each step is 1200/n cents. To see how well that grid serves music, ask how closely it can approximate the intervals the harmonic series actually produces:

step size          = 1200 / n
best approximation = round(target / step) × step
error              = approximation − target

The two targets that matter most, from the harmonic series:

pure fifth       3:2  = 1200 × log2(1.5)  = 701.955 c
pure major third 5:4  = 1200 × log2(1.25) = 386.314 c

Reading that, term by term​

(a) Why round. The grid can only produce multiples of the step, so the best it can do is the nearest multiple to the true interval. round(701.955 / 100) = 7 tells you the fifth in 12-EDO is seven steps.

(b) Why the fifth and third specifically. They're harmonics 3 and 5 — the first two non-octave partials. Approximate those well and triads sound consonant; approximate them badly and the harmony beats audibly.

(c) What counts as "good." Roughly, errors under ~5 cents are inaudible in context; 10–15 cents is noticeable on sustained chords; 20+ is out of tune.

Three readings worth pulling out:

12 is excellent at fifths and mediocre at thirds. Its fifth is off by under 2 cents — imperceptible. Its major third is off by +13.7 cents, seven times worse, and genuinely audible on a sustained major chord. Every equal-tempered triad you have ever heard has a noticeably sharp third; you're simply used to it.

31-EDO nearly perfects the third (+0.78 cents) at the cost of a slightly worse fifth (−5.2). It is very close to quarter-comma meantone — the temperament Europe actually used for roughly two centuries, listed in the temperament table. 19-EDO similarly approximates third-comma meantone. The historical temperaments were not primitive attempts at 12-EDO; they were different priorities, and the EDO grid that matches them is not 12.

53-EDO is nearly exact on both — its fifth is off by 0.07 cents, about 29× more accurate than 12-EDO's, with a very good third as well. On the numbers it is not a close contest.

24-EDO adds nothing harmonically. Its fifth and third are identical to 12-EDO's, because 24 contains 12. Quarter-tones buy melodic inflection, not better consonance — relevant to how the Arabic 24-tone notation is actually used.

3. Why these numbers and not others​

The good rows aren't scattered randomly; they're the denominators of the continued-fraction convergents of log2(3/2) — 2, 5, 12, 41, 53 — the same list that governed where the machine stops.

That is not a coincidence, it's the same fact twice:

"q fifths ≈ p octaves"  ⟺  "p/q ≈ log2(3/2)"  ⟺  "q-EDO has a good fifth"

A chain of fifths closing neatly after q steps and a q-step grid approximating the fifth well are the same statement. So one continued fraction predicts both the viable scale sizes and the viable equal divisions. 19, 22, 31 are strong for the third instead — they'd fall out of running the same analysis on log2(5/4).

4. None of this was unknown​

The alternatives weren't overlooked for lack of discovery. The chronology is humbling:

WhenWhoWhat
~239 BCELüshi Chunqiu (China)12 pitch pipes by the sanfen sunyi (三分損益) fifth-stacking method — 12 reached independently of Greece
78–37 BCEJing Fang (京房)Extended to 60 lü and identified that 53 fifths nearly close — the 53 convergent, ~1700 years before Europe
1555VicentinoBuilt the archicembalo, a ~31-tone keyboard with 36 keys per octave
1577SalinasDescribed 19-tone equal division
1584Zhu Zaiyu (朱載堉)First correct mathematical solution of 12-EDO — computed the 12th root of 2 to 25 digits, by abacus
~1585Simon StevinIndependently derived 12-EDO in Europe
1661HuygensAnalyzed 31-EDO and its relation to meantone
20th c.Partch, Carlos, Blackwood43-tone just scales, non-octave scales, systematic studies of 13–24 EDO

Note the striking gap: 12-EDO's mathematics was solved in 1584, but it did not become standard practice in Europe until roughly the 19th century. The theory was available for 250 years before the practice caught up. Adoption was never about the math.

5. So why did twelve win?​

Four reasons stacking on top of each other. No single one is decisive.

  1. Best fifth per note. Of everything below 41 notes, 12 has by far the best fifth-to-size ratio. Beating it meaningfully requires 41 or 53 keys per octave.
  2. It contains the diatonic set. As file 04 showed, 12 is exactly the closure of the seven-note scale — so it arrives with the entire existing repertoire already playable. 19 and 31 do not.
  3. 12 is divisible by 2, 3, 4 and 6. This is the most underrated reason. Divisibility is what gives you the symmetric structures: the tritone splitting the octave in half, the augmented triad in thirds, the diminished seventh in fourths, the whole-tone scale. Those are compositional tools that Beyond the Basics leans on heavily. 19, 31, 41 and 53 are all prime — they have no internal symmetry, so none of that vocabulary exists in them.
  4. Lock-in. Once instruments, notation, pedagogy and repertoire were welded to 12, switching cost dominated every theoretical advantage. This is the same argument as the Jankó keyboard: isomorphic layouts decisively win the transposition argument and still went nowhere.

Reason 3 deserves emphasis because it's a genuine musical advantage rather than an accident, and it partly redeems 12's mediocre third: what 12 loses in consonance it gains in structure.

6. Takeaway​

  • Score an equal division by how well it approximates the pure fifth (701.955c) and pure third (386.314c).
  • 12-EDO: fifth −1.96c (excellent), third +13.69c (audibly sharp). Every equal-tempered major chord has a sharp third.
  • 31-EDO nearly perfects the third (+0.78) ≈ quarter-comma meantone; 53-EDO is ~29× more accurate than 12 on the fifth. 24-EDO adds nothing harmonically over 12.
  • The good divisions are the continued-fraction convergents of log2(3/2) — the same list that produced the viable scale sizes. One piece of math, two results.
  • The alternatives were all known: Jing Fang found 53 around 40 BCE; Zhu Zaiyu solved 12-EDO in 1584, ~250 years before Europe adopted it.
  • Twelve won on best fifth per note + contains the diatonic + divisibility by 2/3/4/6 (symmetric harmony, impossible in prime divisions) + lock-in.
