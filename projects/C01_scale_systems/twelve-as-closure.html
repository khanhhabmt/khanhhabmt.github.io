Twelve as Closure, Not as a Scale

Twelve passed the two-step-size test in file 02, but failed the landmark test in file 03 as badly as a scale can. That combination is the tell: twelve is not a scale. It's a different kind of object, arrived at for a different reason, and this file says what.

1. The step sizes collapse​

Compare how differentiated the two step sizes are at each stopping point:

n = 7    steps 203.9 and 90.2   →  ratio 203.9 / 90.2  = 2.26
n = 12   steps 113.7 and 90.2   →  ratio 113.7 / 90.2  = 1.26

At seven, the big step is more than twice the small one. "Tone" and "semitone" are unmistakably different objects, so where the two small steps sit becomes the scale's identity — that's the 2 2 1 2 2 2 1 fingerprint, and it's what lets you hear your position.

At twelve, the two sizes are within 26% of each other. Play them in sequence and almost nobody can hear which is which. The scale is effectively uniform, so there is nothing to navigate by — the failure 03 diagnosed via the interval vector, seen from the step side.

One detail that ties the whole section together: the difference between those two steps at n = 12 is

113.7 − 90.2 = 23.46 cents

which is exactly the Pythagorean comma. The gap that H01's derivation computes from (3/2)^12 / 2^7 is the same quantity, reached from the other direction. It isn't an extra fact about tuning bolted on later — it's the leftover unevenness in the twelve-note scale itself, and it's why the two steps very nearly merged.

2. So what is twelve?​

Here's the reframe:

Twelve is the smallest set of notes that contains the seven-note diatonic scale starting from every possible note.

Twelve isn't a scale; it's the closure of the diatonic scale under transposition — the supply cabinet you need so the real scale is available in any key.

You can watch it accumulate. Start with C major and move to the nearest key each time (up a fifth, the smallest possible change — one note):

C major   C D E F G A B                    7 notes
G major   G A B C D E F♯     + F♯          8
D major   D E F♯ G A B C♯    + C♯          9
A major   … + G♯                          10
E major   … + D♯                          11
B major   … + A♯                          12   ← set is now closed

Each new key adds exactly one new note, because adjacent keys on the circle of fifths differ by one note — that's the same adjacency H01 builds its whole map on. Twelve steps, twelve notes, and the cabinet is full. Nothing further is ever needed.

Note what this says about causality: seven is primary, twelve is derived. Twelve exists because you wanted seven in every key.

3. Notation still admits this​

If twelve were the fundamental set, we'd have twelve letter names. We don't:

  • Seven letters, A through G, then it repeats.
  • The other five notes have no names of their own — they're borrowed, written as modifications (F♯, B♭) of the seven.
  • The staff has seven positions per octave (lines and spaces alternating), not twelve. Accidentals are annotations layered on top.
  • Key signatures exist to declare which seven of the twelve are currently in play — a concept that only makes sense if seven is the working set.

Western notation is a seven-note system with a patch applied. That's the historical order fossilized in the writing system, and it's the strongest everyday evidence that twelve came second.

By contrast, a piano keyboard looks like a twelve-note instrument, but even it gives seven notes the white keys and five notes the black ones — the asymmetry that gives pianists their mindset is this same history, made physical.

4. How it actually happened: musica ficta​

The accumulation above is the logical story. The historical one is messier and ends in the same place.

Medieval European music was modal — a seven-note diatonic set, no key changes in the modern sense. Then singers started needing notes outside it, for two practical reasons:

  • Avoiding the tritone. The F–B tritone — the very interval 03 identified as the key's fingerprint — was considered unsingable and was nicknamed diabolus in musica. The fix was to flatten B, giving B♭ as the first accidental in Western music. The two versions were called B molle (soft) and B durum (hard), and their written symbols are the direct ancestors of today's ♭ and ♮.
  • Raising leading tones. Cadences sound stronger with a semitone approach from below, so singers sharpened the note under the final — adding F♯, C♯, G♯ as needed.

These were performance adjustments, often unwritten, applied by convention — hence musica ficta, "feigned music." Over centuries they were absorbed into notation, keyboards grew the corresponding black keys, and the set closed at twelve. Nobody designed the chromatic scale; it precipitated out of two recurring practical needs.

5. And this is where the trouble starts​

The moment twelve exists as a fixed set, the machine that produced it turns hostile. Twelve fifths should close the loop — but

(3/2)^12 = 129.746…      seven octaves = 2^7 = 128

they miss, by the 23.46-cent comma that showed up in §1 as the leftover step unevenness. So a fixed twelve-note instrument cannot have pure fifths and play in every key. Something must be detuned deliberately.

That is precisely the handoff point to the rest of the site:

  • Circle of Fifths → Why the Ring Closes — the comma derived in full, and how equal temperament forces the ring shut.
  • String Instruments → The Comma & Temperament — the competing policies for hiding it, on real instruments.

This section produced the twelve. Those sections deal with the bill.

6. Takeaway​

  • At n = 12 the two step sizes collapse to a 1.26 ratio (vs 2.26 at n = 7) — effectively uniform, no landmarks. Their difference is the Pythagorean comma, 23.46 cents.
  • Twelve is therefore not a scale. It's the closure of the diatonic set under transposition — the minimum supply for playing seven notes in any key.
  • Adding keys one fifth at a time adds exactly one note each time; twelve steps fill the cabinet.
  • Seven is primary, twelve is derived — and notation still shows it: seven letters, seven staff positions, five notes with only borrowed names.
  • Historically it arrived through musica ficta: B♭ to dodge the tritone (ancestor of the ♭ and ♮ signs), sharps to strengthen cadences.
  • Once twelve is fixed, the fifth-chain refuses to close — which is where temperament begins.
